# Example: Fixed-Price Audit Package

This example is based on a neutral synthetic consulting case used for compatibility testing. It is abbreviated for readability.

## Prompt

```text
Use the 5 Persona Advisory Board skill.

Decision: Should a small consulting business offer one fixed-price audit package first, or start with custom proposals for every client?

Constraints: The founder has limited time, no sales team, and needs cash quickly. The risks are underpricing the work, confusing buyers, and building a service that is hard to deliver repeatedly.

Current leaning: Start with one fixed-price audit package, then offer custom implementation only after the audit.
```

## Condensed Output

### Decision

Start with one fixed-price audit package, then offer custom implementation only after the audit.

### Context Read

Stage: execution and offer validation. The founder has limited time, no sales team, and needs cash quickly. The main risks are underpricing, buyer confusion, and creating a service that cannot be delivered repeatedly.

### Selected Lens Views

Product Clarity Lens:

- View: One fixed audit is easier to understand, sell, remember, and refer.
- Blind spot: A generic "audit" can sound vague unless the outcome is sharp.
- Recommendation: Package it around a specific buyer pain and result, not around your process.

Risk and Capital Lens:

- View: Fixed-price creates cash faster and reduces sales waste, but only if scope is tightly controlled.
- Blind spot: Underpricing can turn quick revenue into low-margin chaos.
- Recommendation: Price with a buffer, define exclusions, and cap meetings, revisions, and deliverables.

Scale and Systems Lens:

- View: A repeatable audit can become the intake system.
- Blind spot: If every audit becomes custom behind the scenes, it is only bespoke consulting with a nicer label.
- Recommendation: Use the same intake, checklist, scoring system, report structure, and implementation menu every time.

### Disagreement

The lenses mostly agree on audit-first. The main disagreement is pricing flexibility. Offer Strength wants a clean, easy yes. Risk and Capital warns that fixed price is dangerous without protected scope. Scale and Systems adds that the package only works if delivery is truly repeatable.

### Future Self Check

The bigger regret is probably not "I started too simple." The bigger regret is building a messy custom service that consumes founder time, confuses buyers, and cannot be sold consistently.

### Final Decision Brief

- Decision: Start with one fixed-price audit package.
- Why: It is clearer to buy, faster to sell, easier to deliver repeatedly, and creates a natural path into higher-value custom implementation.
- Risk: Underpricing and loose scope can turn the audit into unpaid custom strategy work.
- Next action: Today, the founder writes a one-page audit offer: target buyer, painful problem, fixed price, timeline, deliverables, exclusions, and implementation upsell path. Use it on the next 3-5 qualified sales conversations before revising.
- Do not do: Do not write custom proposals for every prospect before you have diagnosed their problem through the paid audit.
