## 🧠 Question 1: The Cost & The Hard Path (Choose the Option that Fits Best)

Here are three real alternative stories from your actual journey, updated with your correct timeline:

### 🎯 Option A: The Tech Wilderness & Return to building (Bootcamp to NPower to Flooring)
*   **The Story:** You completed the UNC Full-Stack Bootcamp in June 2022, but hit a frozen job market. You spent a long time out of active tech. Refusing to let that dream go, you joined NPower in late 2024, fast-tracking your Google IT certs (Aug/Sept 2024) and CompTIA A+ (Dec 2024). In May 2025, you started working in physical construction labor for your dad's business, CRG Flooring, which immediately sparked the realization that you could combine your tech skills with real-world business needs.
*   **The Cost:** $15,000 bootcamp tuition, years of career transition, and the mental weight of trying to find your place in tech.
*   **Who thought it was the wrong call:** Family or close friends who saw you walk away from your initial tech training in 2022, only to go back to studying entry-level certifications in 2024 and then start heavy physical labor in May 2025. They thought you were jumping between unrelated paths.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"I graduated a coding bootcamp in 2022 but hit a brick wall in the job market. I spent a long time in transition, but in late 2024, I rebuilt my foundation through NPower and got my CompTIA. In May 2025, I started working in physical construction labor for my dad's business, which is what actually triggered me to build Kenbun to solve real business logistics."*

### 🎯 Option B: The 15-Month Flooring & Code Grind (Hardwood by day, Code by night)
*   **The Story:** Since May of 2025 (for a little over a year), you have been working in physical labor for CRG Flooring. Hauling thousands of pounds of hardwood up stairs and laying down floors is exhausting. Instead of collapsing after a 10-hour shift, you spent your nights self-directing your learning—building local server stacks, configuring docker databases, and writing Python automation scripts.
*   **The Cost:** Extreme physical exhaustion, giving up your personal free time/evenings, and working with zero external boss holding you accountable to code.
*   **Who thought it was the wrong call:** Anyone who saw you spent at 10 PM, wondering why you didn't just sleep or relax instead of coding local databases and server orchestration pipelines at 1 AM.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"Since May of 2025, I’ve been hauling hardwood floors by day and coding our business systems at night. It’s been a brutal, exhausting year of manual labor and self-directed building. My family and friends saw how tired I was and thought I was overworking myself, but I was committed to proving that I could build production-ready systems."*

### 🎯 Option C: AI Systems vs. Easy Freelance Web Design (Turning down quick cash)
*   **The Story:** When you started building tech for CRG Flooring in May 2025, you could have taken the easier path of building simple static websites (landing pages) for other businesses for quick cash. But you committed to solving the much harder, unglamorous problem of automated AI lead systems and local database stacks (Kenbun) for CRG Flooring.
*   **The Cost:** Immediate freelance cash flow. You spent months coding databases, routing scripts, and troubleshooting LLM hallucinations on a local server without immediate payout.
*   **Who thought it was the wrong call:** Clients or family who wanted you to just make quick, simple websites for fast money instead of staying up until 1:00 AM trying to fix "hallucination thresholds" on local docker containers.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"I could have made easy cash building simple static websites for local contractors. That was the easy path. Instead, I committed to building custom AI automation pipelines and database syncs for my dad's business starting in May 2025. It cost me immediate freelance money and spent months of my time, and people around me didn't understand why I was spending weeks trying to solve 'LLM hallucinations' instead of just building simple pages."*

## 🛠️ Question 2: The Messy Reality & Overcoming It (The Hallucination Wall)
*   **Prompt:** *A stretch when something you cared about got harder, messier, or more disappointing than you expected. What happened, and why you stayed with it.*
*   **Specific Example:** **Building the Flooring IT automation from scratch.**
*   **What happened:** You expected to easily connect API endpoints and let the LLM handle client scheduling and invoicing. Instead, the raw LLMs hallucinated wildly—generating incorrect material estimates and wrong scheduling dates.
*   **The Messy Reality:** You had no pre-made framework and no developer team. It was incredibly frustrating because you realized you couldn't trust the AI with your dad's business reputation.
*   **Why you stayed:** You realized that if you could solve the reliability gap, you could protect the trade subcontractors from losing money. You persisted, designed the local server setup (`p330`), and built a System 2 supervisor validation loop to audit and test the code before it ran.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"When I started, the AI was hallucinating crucial invoice details. I had no framework. It was messy and disappointing, but I realized that to make AI useful for real-world operations, I had to build safety gates. That's why I stayed with it and built a multi-agent auditing loop."*

## ⏳ Question 3: Self-Accountability & The Grind (The 99-Day Streak)
*   **Prompt:** *Something you kept doing well after it stopped being new or exciting, with no external accountability keeping you at it.*
*   **Specific Example:** **Your 99-Day Journaling & Code Streak.**
*   **The Grind:** Sitting down at night after a brutal day on a construction site (like a frustrating shoe-mold installation that took twice as long as expected).
*   **No one watching:** No boss, client, or teacher was checking if you logged in. You kept coding local database schemas, writing telemetry sync scripts, and debugging Next.js UI rendering issues alone in your room.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"I kept a 99-day streak of coding and planning at night after laying floors all day. No one was checking my work. I had to force myself to write unit tests and manage databases at 1 AM when I was physically exhausted."*

## 📋 Question 4: Realistic Expectations (The Glamour vs. The Work)
*   **Prompt:** *What excites you about a role like this, and what worries you about it.*
*   **What excites you:** Giving local organizations and non-profits the technical leverage they usually can't afford—building custom tools that actually save them hours.
*   **What worries you:** Day-to-day work is mostly unglamorous. It's not drafting high-level AI theories; it’s cleaning messy CSV files, fixing broken API keys, and handling network timeouts. You also worry about **maintenance**—building something cool that the non-profit fails to use because they don't have tech staff.
*   **Key Phrase:** *"I’m excited about building tools that give non-profits real leverage. But I know the day-to-day is mostly unglamorous data cleaning and API debugging. My biggest worry is post-launch adoption—ensuring the team can actually maintain the system after the fellowship ends."*
